A video of a stroke only helps if you can stop it on the right frame and measure what you see. This guide walks through the whole routine in AngleMotion, from loading a clip to angles, markup and distances. Every step below is a button you can press today, and you can follow along on your own footage during the free hour at the end.

## 1. Load a clip and find the moment

Press **Upload Video** and choose a clip of your player. If you would rather practise first, **Demo (Tutorial)** loads a clip to follow along with.

Press play to watch the stroke (the space bar does the same). Then press or drag along the timeline to jump to the moment you want to study: the load, contact, the finish. On a long clip, pinch the timeline, or Ctrl-scroll, to zoom in.

To land on one exact frame, step forward a frame at a time. The arrow keys step too.

## 2. Switch on the skeleton

Everything for measuring lives under **Metrics**. Open it and choose **Skeleton**. The AI skeleton finds the player's joints on every frame. The "Skeleton on / off" row inside is the one switch that turns it off again.

Check that it found your player. If the skeleton sits over the right person, answer **Yes**. If it does not, answer **No** and then click your player.

## 3. Take a first reading with AI Detect Angles

Back in Metrics, **AI Detect Angles** reads the pose on the current frame and measures it for you: elbows, knees, and hips against shoulders. The angles appear in the data column beside the player, and the frame is kept as a snapshot. Press play and you are back to live video; the snapshot stays saved.

Treat this as a first draft. Every reading is yours to edit, and you stay in charge of what it means.

## 4. Measure an angle yourself

Open **Metrics**, then **Draw**, and pick the **Angle arrow**. It is an arrow that reads its own direction as an angle. Press at the joint and drag outwards along the forearm, the thigh or the shoulder line. When you let go, the angle is written on the arrow.

## 5. Compare two angles

**Angle differential** takes the next two arrows you draw and logs the difference between them: hips against shoulders, or upper arm against forearm. Draw the first arrow along the hips, then the second along the shoulders. Both angles and the difference between them land in the data column.

## 6. Mark up the frame

Pick **Pen** to circle a contact point or sketch a swing path. Line, Arrow, Circle and Rectangle sit right beside it. **Style** sets the colour and thickness of your next mark and also restyles any mark you have already drawn. **Select** lets you move what you drew, and Undo puts it back.

## 7. Measure real distances with the ruler

The **Ruler** sits near the bottom of the Draw list. It needs one length it knows. Pick a frame where the whole racket is visible, choose **Racket** as the reference (most adult rackets are 68.6 cm, or 27 in), click the butt cap at the bottom of the handle and then the tip of the frame. The panel turns green and says Calibrated.

Now drag from one point to another to measure the gap between the knees, the length of a stride or the height of contact. You can switch units between cm and m or ft and in. The calibration stays with this clip when you change tools; calibrate again if the camera moves.

## Where your work goes

Angles, comparisons and markup all land in the screenshot, the recording and the player's report, so the analysis you do in one session becomes part of the player's record.

## A note on the AI

AngleMotion is a coaching tool, and the coach decides. The skeleton and the angle readings are drafts for you to check and adjust. What the numbers mean for a player's technique is a coaching judgement, not something the software tells you.
